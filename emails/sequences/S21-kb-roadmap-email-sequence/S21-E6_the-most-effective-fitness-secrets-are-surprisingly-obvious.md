# The MOST Effective Fitness “SECRETS” are Surprisingly Obvious

Hello {first_name},

 

Watching gymnasts is amazing. They make everything look easy which is a paradox, because what they are doing is incredibly difficult. 

 

For example, watching men on the rings hold an Iron Cross (a movement fairly common in the gymnastics world) - may have made many of us men feel like we can pull a move off like that…only to find out when we try at our local neighborhood park, how wildly difficult it is LOL.

 

No gymnastics coach ever told their athlete to go do an Iron Cross as a beginner. Instead, they worked on progressions sustained over time, to the point where holding an Iron Cross was possible. 

 

Kettlebell Lifestyle follows the same essentail principle. 

 

Lay the Foundation > Build > Perform > Sustain

 

Here’s an example straight from the course illustrating a basic progression (I will explain further as you read on):

 

LEVEL 1 (Beginner Track)

Side of Hip Raises


Static Alternating Side Lunge

Alternating Halo Side Lunge Combo

 

Progression Explained:

The glute medius is targeted in the Side of Hip Raises which is important to stabilize the hip in single leg and lateral movement. Essential for ANYTHING athletic. The Static Alternating Side Lunge moves from an isolated movement to an integrated movement.  The Alternating Halo Side Lunge Combo adds weight, integrates the upper and lower body, becomes more dynamic, trains balance, coordination, timing, etc.

 

 

LEVEL 2 (Intermediate Track)

Hand to Hand Swing

 

Single Arm Clean

 

RMT (Rotational Movement Training) Clean

Progression Explained:

The Swing itself is a ballistic hinge movement and is the basis of many advanced movements in Kettlebell lifting. Adding the Hand to Hand Swing is a ballistic hinge movement that works coordination, gets you comfortable with timing, and works single-arm training in preparation for the Clean. The Clean is a BIG step in integrating the upper and lower body and a chance to practice "hand insertion". The RMT (rotational movement) Clean takes each of these elements up a notch with more rotation, coordination, timing, and significantly more integration between the upper and lower body.

 

The Bottom Line

 

Progressions yield better results, period. Even if you are already strong and used to tough workouts, following a progression rather than just diving in the deep end works better in the long-run - trust me.
