Movement is good for everybody. 

 

This is not a controversial statement.

 

Add to that the idea of “use it or lose it” and it’s even more obvious why some sort of movement practice is not just helpful, but necessary if you want to live to your full human potential. 

 

Thousands of years ago consistent movement was baked into everyday life. There was ZERO need to think about it for most people just like most of us don’t have to think about going to work. It’s how we survive so we do it. 

 

That said, many of us get bogged down on what kind of exercise to do. 

 

So how do you choose what type of exercise?

 

One way is to look around at what other people do and see how their results are.

 

          Some are committed to heavy weights, often done at commercial gyms.

          Then there are endurance activities like cycling, running, swimming, etc. 

          CrossFit is another popular style of mixed training.

          At-home bodyweight focused training is common as well.

 

Now take a closer look. Regardless of the type of exercise someone enjoys, the people who are able to keep it up for years have one thing in common -  they know how to recover. 

 

Recovery is the key.

 

Without recovery you’ll end up an achy, injured, stiff person who has more trouble getting around and doing normal activities. Interestingly, that’s true whether someone trains a lot before their body gives out or doesn’t train at all and loses it through lack of use. 

 

Here’s the point, if you learn how to build recovery into your training, you are golden REGARDLESS of the type of training you find enjoyable. 

 

  • Powerlifting
  • Olympic lifting
  • Bodyweight training
  • Biking
  • Swimming
  • Long walks on the beach
  • Kettlebells
  • Bodybuilding
  • Running
  • Martial arts or MMA

All of these (and others) are great. You should perform the type of exercise you enjoy…as long as you recover from it.

 

Kettlebell Lifestyle is a roughly 9 week program (it can be longer depending on how you use it, and it’s perfect for going through the program multiple times) that ingrains recovery principles into you. 

 

By the end of the program you’ll know what to do so your body can recover and positively adapt to whatever form of training you choose to do.
