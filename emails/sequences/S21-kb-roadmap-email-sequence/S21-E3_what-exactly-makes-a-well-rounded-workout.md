# What Exactly Makes a WELL-ROUNDED Workout?

Hello {first_name},

 

Did you know that people who forage for wild foods and herbs will take some from an area, but they’ll be careful to leave plenty behind so there will be great harvests year after year?

 

There are many parallels here with regards to our fitness....

 

Your body is its own ecosystem. If you deplete its energy week after week, it won’t be able to produce like it could before. 

 

But if you harvest responsibly, you’ll have energy whenever you need it. 

 

That’s why I choose to workout intelligently and methodically, because it means I’ll be able to push it - whenever I want or need to. 

 

So what exactly goes into well-rounded workouts (regardless of your goal) so you can stay strong and feel great for decades instead of just your 20s? 

 

 

The 4 Elements of Well-Rounded Training

 

The exercises, weights, and volume will change based on your goal, however the fundamentals stay the same. 

 

Personalization 

At its core, personalization is about taking responsibility for your continued progress. You learn what signs and symptoms to pay attention to and how to modify training so that your training stress doesn’t overtake your ability to recover. Learning to see how life outside the gym affects your training AND exactly how to adjust your training to continue to make progress is key.  

 

Preparation: A Core Temperature & Joint Warm Up

Warm ups are hugely underrated. A core temperature warm up gradually raises body temperature, circulating blood from the heart to the tissues, generating warmth, as well as exercising the respiratory system. 

 

A quality joint warm up prepares the proprioceptors (directly tied to the intelligence of your tissues such as ligaments and promotes body awareness) and triggers your joints to release synovial fluid (joint lubricant). In addition, we use movements to increase specific body control and range of motion where one part of the body is fixed, (for example the lower body) while another part (upper body in this case) is performing more targeted movements. 

 

The spine and ribs have tons of small muscles, tendons, ligaments and joints. If these joints are tight they essentially get "bound" together, leading to compensations. In regular English, your body will allow you to get your arms over your head even if doing so is like running your car without oil. The joint warm up keeps these joints and tissues from being stiff and bound up which means you’ll feel better and move better. 

 

This does not take but a few minutes to perform and will pay you dividends if you perform it consistently.

 

Skill Based Training

In my programs, proper biomechanics, methodical progressions, and the integration of the nervous system are how I develop skill. You’ll not only train strength, you’ll practice the skill of being strong with precision and accuracy. 

 

Knowing the feel of proper biomechanics and honing the ability to express strength with precision enables effective transfer to other tools and other sports. Not to mention that being skilled in your movement avoids unnecessary wear and tear on your body. 

 

Recovery & Cool Down

After your workout you have the opportunity to guide your body from the stress and sympathetic state (fight or flight) of training to one that is more parasympathetic (rest & digest) where you can recover. The more time you spend in recovery mode, the better your results will be. 

 

The tight, achy bodies so many people have is because they haven’t learned how to bring their bodies back to a more de-stressed baseline. The emphasis on breathing, working in, skill development days, and targeted stretches are all part of cooling down and bringing your body into balance. 

 

Maybe you're saying, "Mike…I don’t have time for all that."

 

Including each of these 4 elements into your training is what makes the difference between someone looking back on their glory days and someone else who is a badass throughout the aging process.
