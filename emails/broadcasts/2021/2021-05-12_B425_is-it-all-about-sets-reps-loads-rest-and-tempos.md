# Is it ALL about sets, reps, loads, rest, and tempos?

Hi {first_name},

 

I remember years ago, when I looked at my fitness and competition goals, with so many of them, I thought this:

 

"If I’m consistent, train my a$$ off, and hit all my numbers (meaning I completed the sets, reps, loads, rest, and tempos my coach laid out for me) then I'd feel great, be happy, and successful.

 

Well, interestingly enough, a number of things happened…

I hit some goals
I didn’t hit others
I felt good about myself most times and built confidence
I also felt sh*tty often and would beat myself mentally and emotionally into the ground for "failures" and any small imperfection
I rarely ever missed a workout
I ended up overtraining 
I learned a TON about pushing myself, and also trusting the process, even when uncertainty was present 

All of this is merely to highlight that whether our goals are performance-based, health, aesthetic, or some combination of all three, to be fit AND feel great, we simply cannot treat our bodies like machines. 

 

There are MANY ways to improve and see progress in other key areas. 

 

 

Here are some questions to reflect on in relation to your workouts:

 

Are you noticing you have more energy to put into family time?
Are you finding you are enjoying your workouts more?
Are you feeling more grounded and centered in your communications?
Do you wake up in the morning with less joint stiffness?
Are you more motivated to eat more regularly and more whole foods?

 

If you answered yes, to any of the above, right on! And if not, now may be a good time to re-assess with fresh eyes what your current workout is doing for you outside of the usual measurements of success. 

 

I hope today's email provided you some guidance or inspiration for looking at how what we choose to do in the gym has a direct impact on the things that also matter to us outside of it. Training has the ability to make us better people at the end of the day, which I've found has much less to do with the exact number of reps, sets, loads, rest, and tempo, and instead a deeper level of honest introspection. 

 

 

In health,
