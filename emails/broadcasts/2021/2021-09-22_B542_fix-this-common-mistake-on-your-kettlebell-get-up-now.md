# Fix this common mistake on your Kettlebell Get Up now

Hi {first_name},

 

I recall for a long time early on I simply couldn't figure out when moving through the Turkish Get Up (especially during one part in particular), why one of my shoulders would feel unstable, "jammed-up", and lack stability.

 

I came to realize that this point of transition (into what is often called "Elbow Post") is a common challenge many others face as well.

 

The Turkish Get Up offers us a way to move a load from the floor into a standing position safely. It has numerous benefits, such as multi-positional stability, strength, and mobility of the shoulders and hips, plus great core integration, and more. Each of these benefits has the ability to aid in the preparation and conditioning for many work or sports environments.

 

While there are a range of variations to the Get Up, no matter which one you choose (here is a video I did with Mind Pump Media showing 3 different TGU variations), the mistake(s) I describe below apply to nearly all of them.

 

Common Mistakes:

During the raising phase up to the Elbow Post (either of the below):

Base shoulder hikes up too close to the ear
Head and neck projects forward to initiate the movement 

 

During the lowering phase down from the Straight Arm Post:

The planted arm is positioned too close to the hip as it is lowered down to the elbow (causing a bunching up of the body and oftentimes a shift in body position, away from where you started).

 

A Few Tip(s) to Explore:

During the raising phase up to the Elbow Post:

Drive off the heel of the planted leg
Allow torso to rotate slightly (as a result of the leg drive)
Guide kettlebell along a diagonal line towards the belly button 
Drive planted elbow into the ground as the chest lifts

 

During the lowering phase down from the Straight Arm Post:

Readjust the planted hand as needed to create enough space to descend to the elbow with proper positioning
