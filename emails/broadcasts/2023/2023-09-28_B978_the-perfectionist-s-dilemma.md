# The Perfectionist's Dilemma

Hi {first_name},

 

If you're like me, sometimes living out perfectionistic tendencies is a double-edged sword. In some ways, it can feel incredibly stressful, like life requires a ton of self-responsibility to do things the "right way", and at other times it can indeed be a superpower.

 

Today, we'll be unpacking the perfectionist's dilemma...so get ready ; )

 

In my personal experience and coaching around perfectionism, I've found understanding the Ennegram to be incredibly helpful. The Ennegram encompasses 9 distinct personality types that we all possess to varying degrees, based on our life experiences since childhood.

 

Within these 9 types, there's one known as our Core Type, which serves as the driving force behind our unconscious motivations in life. This core type is fueled by a fundamental fear, belief, and desire, 

 

It's the lens through which we view ourselves, and the world.

 

One of the biggest insights I've gained and use in my coaching practice to determine someone's unique psychological fingerprint (using the Inner Compass System), is that how we draw upon the other eight types serves as coping or compensation strategies to manage the fundamental fear, belief, and desire of our Core Type.

 

Now that we've touched on a few Enneagram basics, let's return to the Perfectionist's dilemma.

 

The Perfectionist corresponds to Type 1 in the Enneagram system, also known as the Improver, Fixer, Moralist, and Reformer, to name a few.

 

As with all types, there are inherent pros and cons (light and shadow), with varying levels of functionality that we shift between throughout the day, depending on life's challenges and our current state.

 

Generally speaking, individuals who lead as a Core Type 1 are conscientious, responsible, disciplined, orderly, and serious.

 

Their motivations are as follows:

 

FEAR of making mistakes, being imperfect, or being "bad".
DESIRE to be good, virtuous, and have integrity.
CORE BELIEF centered around being good or okay if they do what's right (according to their defined values, and inner code).

 

At their best, they are discerning, wise, principled, and fair, but they can sometimes fall into traps of rigidity, perfectionism, criticism, and judgment (of self/others)

 

I have a deep connection with this Enneagram type because I've learned to get my needs met (physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual) by leading with it at my core.

 

While the Inner Compass System, and the Enneagram, offer far more depth than I can share here today, even a basic understanding is incredibly empowering for personal growth and healing.
