# Becoming Comfortable with Discomfort

Hi {first_name},

 

I understand that most people do not want to experience discomfort. And in many respects, I share that same sentiment.

 

BUT I've noticed there can be more to it than that if we so choose...

 

Over the years, parts of me have learned not only to see the value in discomfort, but also see its beauty and even enjoy it at times LOL.

 

Through discomfort, through adversity, and through testing our character, we can learn a great deal about ourselves and have a rare opportunity to rise up.

 

 

One of the keys, however, to using discomfort in a healthier, more productive way has to do with bringing intention into the equation.

 

Whether it’s public speaking for the first time, asking someone out on a date, taking a new martial arts class, playing an instrument, or learning a foreign language - whatever it is that makes you uncomfortable, offers a path to growth.

 

The next time there is something that you would like to explore but are experiencing fear or hesitation around it, I invite you to consider any or all of these questions:

 

Why do I, or parts of me, want to do this (be as honest as you can with yourself here)?
What would success look AND feel like?
How will other aspects of my life become better as a result of this?
Do I have anyone I can reach out to if I need support?
What tools do I have when things get hard?
On this one, I’ll remind you of something that we all have, that doesn’t cost any money, and something that is the anchor to helping us navigate any challenging experience - BREATH. 
Tip: Breathe low (belly), slow, and light (keep it nasal).

These questions have helped me more times than I can count to intentionally enter into discomfort and utilize it in a more productive manner.

 

Wishing you a great rest of your day,
