# Underestimated strength: my favorite grip training exercise

Hello {first_name},

 

A powerful grip is truly practical strength. One of the primary ways we interact with the world is through our hands. 

 

If you need to express strength, chances are your grip is going to be an important aspect. The trouble is, most gym work isn’t that great at building a strong grip. 

 

If you’ve done heavy deadlifts before, you know that the first thing to give out is grip. That’s why people use lifting straps or a special grip for heavy deadlifts. If your hands start to slip, your brain compensates and reduces the force you can apply. 

 

Grip is a HUGE limiting factor. 

 

Now consider grip from the reverse perspective. If you find yourself in a situation where you have to grab someone to prevent them from doing harm to you or others, your grip strength is an unmistakable broadcast. Even before conscious thought has time to become words, they receive the signal of how powerful your grip is loud and clear. 

 

Talk about functional strength. 

 

My Favorite Way To Train Grip

 

I like dynamic movements. Working grip from every angle with special tools doesn’t appeal to me. Instead, I use a tool designed for wrestlers (a very grip intensive sport) so I’m building grip at the same time as I train all the other muscles to be scrappy and tough. 

 

The Bulgarian Bag swing - when done for higher reps - will work your grip like nothing else. The leather handle starts to get slippery and you have to crush the handles or it will go flying. 

 

Ask anyone who has trained with a Bulgarian Bag how their hands and forearms felt after some high rep swings. (They’ll tell you the grip training is amazing.)

 

A strong grip will also protect your shoulders and elbows when training with kettlebells, barbells, and pull up bars.
