# 3 tips that will take your stretching to that next level

Hi {first_name},
 

I received a great response from last week’s kettlebell email “Push Pressing Your Way to the Top.” Here’s another one on a topic that I’m excited to share with you - stretching and flexibility!

 

No matter which stretches you are performing to improve posture, performance, or recovery, below are 3 things you can do to immediately make big strides in your personal stretching practice. 

 

But, first let’s briefly discuss what flexibility is and how it differs from mobility - two terms that both relate to the topic of stretching that often get confused with one another.

 

Generally speaking, flexibility refers to the ability of a muscle to lengthen (more so passive in nature).  This differs from mobility - mobility is the combination of flexibility AND strength through a given range of motion. Mobility therefore integrates the nervous system, and body control is the desired end result. This element of control is very important to reduce injury risk, as well as help you become a more functional and resilient mover. 

 

 

So what are the 3 things you can do to improve both your flexibility AND mobility right now?

 

1. Raising Your Core Temperature Before Stretching 

 

The tissues of our body respond very well to heat. Thus performing stretches when the body is warm can go a long way to helping you get more out of them, and also reduce your risk of injury. I enjoy performing a brief core temperature warm up (for example, a brisk walk, marching in place, an easy row, etc. for around to 4-10 minutes). The idea here is to gradually get a light sweat going, and then move into your stretching routine. Often times I find it beneficial to also wear layers, such as sweat pants and a sweater during this time. 

 

2. Stretching Before Bed

 

I often get asked, “Mike, when is the best time to stretch?” What I’ve found is that stretching immediately following training, as well as before bed are both fantastic and produce noticeable results. Before bed is a critical time because our bodies are under the selective pressures of gravity to a much lesser degree and our postural muscles are not actively engaged. See what happens when you make this a regular part of your evening practice and shoot me a message with how it went!

 

3. Duration & Active Components 

 

a) Duration. In order to have a more substantial long-term effect on the flexibility of our tissues, I suggest holding stretches for longer duration. Usually I recommend approximately 90 seconds total of a stretch (or longer if needed) to start. You can do this in one shot, or break it up into 3 sets of 30 seconds with a short 10 second rest in between. Especially when combined with active components, these longer static stretches tend to produce longer term quality changes in flexibility and mobility. 

 

b) Active Components. Involving the nervous system to build strength and control across a range of motion requires an active component.  When doing this, the effort level doesn’t need to be intense, in fact I find it is best to ramp up the effort gradually during stretches and work closely with the breath.

 

Below is one example of a sequence you can try with any stretch:

 

Find your natural end-range for a given stretch
Relax into it and hold: (~60-90 seconds total)
Contract and activate the muscle you are aiming to stretch: 10-30 sec
Relax and go deeper: 10-30 sec
Contract and activate (starting from this newly acquired end-range): 10-30 sec
Relax & go deeper: 10-30 seconds
Repeat 1-3 more times with the same contract-relax sequence

I hope you found today’s tips on stretching helpful. Remember, quality (as with everything) is always preferred to over quantity. Thus, when performing your stretches, I suggest picking the 2-5 stretches that are likely to have the biggest positive impact on your health and performance, and sticking there for few weeks. If you have additional time on your hands, do this multiple times a day; as your flexibility and mobility improve, gradually add another stretch or two into the mix.
