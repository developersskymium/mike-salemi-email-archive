# Push Pressing Your Way to the Top

Hi {first_name},

As promised last week, here is the email I created just for you where we explore the Kettlebell Push Press.

The Kettlebell Push Press has to be one of my favorite integrated pressing movements. The goal with this exercise is to begin utilizing power from the legs to drive the kettlebell into the Overhead Lockout Position.

 

Today we’ll be going into a step-by-step “how-to” on this movement, along with a few tips. 

 

When first learning, I like to break it up into two variations. 

 

Variation One

Heels Remain Down 

 

 

This first classic Push Press Variation is quite simple. As you dip and drive with the legs to propel the kettlebell upward, your heels will remain down the entire time. This tends to be good for people who are new to the movement entirely and are making their first progression from the Standing Strict Overhead Press, into this more dynamic and full body movement . 

 

Variation Two

Heels Rise

 

 

This second, more advanced variation requires even more total body integration, timing, power, and coordination. One thing to notice on this version is that you’ll be coming up powerfully onto the balls of the feet. This will involve more movement to take place around the ankles, forcing the calves to assist to a greater degree in the force generation sequence.

 

Let’s now break down the steps of this type of Push Press (version two):

 

Set up as for a Standing Overhead Press
While in the Rack Position, it is ideal to depress the loaded shoulder to the point at which the elbow rests on the pelvic/iliac crest, while maintaining a lengthened spine; however, do not force it
Dip body straight down 
Propel kettlebell powerfully into the Overhead Lockout Position with your legs, while extending up onto the balls of your feet
As the kettlebell enters the Overhead Lockout Position, heels should return to the floor
Lower kettlebell back into the Rack Position maintaining control
 

Additional Tips
 

Control the first dip. The first dip is the initial point when the body descends down,  pre-loading the acceleration phase. The idea here is to lower down quickly, however while maintaining control at all times. 

Timing the heels with the lockout. Most people will experience difficulty with the timing involved in this variation of the Push Press. Aim to have the have your heels return back to the floor around the same time as the arm/elbow lock out overhead. 
 
When to press. During a Push Press, majority of the work is done by the legs (via triple extension of the ankles, knees, and hips). It’s only the final ~1/4 of the movement overhead that the arms come in to press the kettlebell into the lockout position. 

 

Toss either Push Press variation one or two into your training and work on the details discussed above and I guarantee you’ll become a more functional overhead athlete as a result : )
