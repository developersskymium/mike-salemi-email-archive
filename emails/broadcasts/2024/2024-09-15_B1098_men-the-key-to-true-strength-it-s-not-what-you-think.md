# Men, The Key to True Strength - It’s Not What You Think

Hi {first_name},

 

There was a time when thinking about strength, all that mattered to me was the number on the bar or a record set.


And for a while, that worked.

 

I saw the fruits of countless hours of hard training, developed solid levels of discipline early on in life, and was celebrated by my peers,

 

But then something started changing...

 

I started maturing.

 

I began to realize that while physical strength is certainly important for a man, there are other forms of strength that are equally, if not more, important.

 

Whether it was friendships ending, the loss of loved ones in the family, financial challenges, or the breakdown of intimate relationships, as I gained more life experience, I realized that my emotional strength, capacity, range, and maturity were areas I needed to foster and develop.

 

The truth is, the weight of emotions can crush a man much harder—and for much longer—than any failed attempt in the gym.

 

That said, there are strong parallels between the physical strength we build in the weight room and the emotional strength we cultivate in life.

 

Both require discipline, courage, a solid plan, commitment, and sometimes the help of others.

 

Yet one of the most overlooked paths for men to build inner strength is through vulnerability.

 

In a world where we're told to "man up" and push through challenges, we forget that one of the greatest acts of courage is facing what's inside of us.

 

We all carry wounds, fears, and insecurities, but many of us bury them under layers of busyness, distraction, or anger.

 

Here’s the key...

True strength isn’t about avoiding discomfort – it’s about confronting it.

 

For men to thrive, we need to develop emotional intelligence. This means:

Recognizing when you're feeling overwhelmed, stressed, or triggered.
Allowing those emotions to be there, rather than suppressing them.
Exploring what's at the root of them.
Learning to work with them in healthy ways – some could involve sharing with another man you trust, doing some breathwork, setting a timer for 60 seconds and giving yourself permission to have a full embodied release of the emotion, experimenting with a particular meditation, or any number of other tools.

Here’s a simple practice you can start today:

Pause when you're feeling emotionally charged.
Ask yourself, "What am I truly feeling right now?"
Name it – is it fear, frustration, sadness, or something else?
Breathe into that feeling without judgment - don't rush this part.
If you're ready, share it with someone you trust.

If you are going to share it, it's good practice to always ask this person (or group of men) first if they'd be open to supporting you. AND be clear about what type of support you are requesting...this could be to simply listen, offer a reflection, or give feedback.

 

This small process and act can create a ripple effect in all areas of your life.

 

It's easy to stay guarded, but the men who truly grow and succeed are those willing to do the inner work.

 

Take a step today. Face the emotions that may have been holding you back. It’s one of the most powerful things you can do for yourself and those around you.

 

With all that said, I also realize how powerful and needed it is to be guided through a process where all parts of you can be welcomed and honed.

 

In a group of men, all there to grow from the inside out just as much as from the outside in, there is deep medicine in that.
