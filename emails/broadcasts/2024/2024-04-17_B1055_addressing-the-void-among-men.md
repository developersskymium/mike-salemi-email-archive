# Addressing the Void Among Men...

Hi {first_name},

 

What do men really need?

 

You may have heard me talk about this before, but it's worth emphasizing: men are in dire need of solid male mentors, role models, and healthy connections with other men.

 

What occurs when a man grows up without a father figure?

 

Considering that the majority of men today are raised by women (and let me be clear, this is not a slight against women or mothers), there's a particular kind of nourishment that only a father can provide.

 

There's a deep wound in the male psyche, as respected author, poet, and men’s work leader Robert Bly would say in the absence of the father. Society as a whole is lacking that paternal presence, and it affects his maturity and sense of wholeness.

 

Maybe a father was physically present but not emotionally available. I see and hear this a lot with the men I work with.

 

Bly describes how there is a collective grief among men because of this absence.

 

If you've had a father, even a good one, chances are high that they weren't there for you in all the ways you wanted or needed. And this isn’t about blaming fathers — it would be nearly impossible for fathers to meet every single one of their child's needs.

 

Additionally, in nearly every society where boys were initiated into men, there needed to be a separation from the father. To put it bluntly, it's about metaphorically "killing the father" to psychologically transition into manhood. Unless we do that, we'll always remain children. Part of the work of individuation is where we learn to father ourselves as men, and make some form of peace with our fathers/father figures.

 

Carl Jung shared that individuation involved becoming your own person, with your own beliefs and ideals that might be separate from those of your parents and society.

 

And that's precisely why men need real men around, to model it, and with us along our journey.

 

What we tend to see in society is children in adult bodies seeking guidance from other children in adult bodies. There are few elders left, and even those who exist often go unnoticed or lack the respect they deserve.

 

After much reflection, this is one of the main reasons why I've started a free online community for men.

 

While there's no financial cost, the price of admission is participation and engagement. There are enough "dead" groups out there. We only grow if we participate, contribute, and become a stand for one another.

 

Every week, I'll be going live on Wednesdays at 10:30 am PST. The calls are archived, and throughout the week, there will be prompts, mini challenges, book explorations, and discussions. 

 

The focus is around education and brotherhood.

 

We’ll see where this journey takes us, but in just two weeks, we've already gathered about 35 members, and I'm loving the energy that is starting to build.

 

I’ll be putting up a poll in the group tomorrow for members to choose the best days and times that work for them to select a group practice call in the next few weeks. This is an added bonus, and I'll be leading you through some of the work I guide men through at our in-person events.

 

Right now, I'm running the group through Facebook to make it simple.

 

If this sounds like something you want to be a part of, click here and scroll down on the Group Sign Up Form, and you’ll go through a brief approval process before joining the group.

 

All my best,
