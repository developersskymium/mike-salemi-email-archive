B1093 · Why Men Lie Awake at Night...

Hi {first_name}

 

A few months back, around 1am, I found myself lying awake in bed. Not because I didn’t have an evening wind-down routine, but rather because, even with that, my mind just wouldn’t stop racing.

 

I was at my limit point with responsibilities—hustling and catching up following busy work travel, helping care for a sick 18 month-old, making an effort to be there for my wife the best I could, and feeling the weight of it all.

 

Even as a coach, there are times when I hit my upper limit of what I can effectively manage. As a result, I, like many men, tend to hold things in.

 

I have a pattern of internalizing my emotions, putting on a facade, so I can "be the strong one." Now, while I love being the leader of my family and I have cultivated a high capacity to hold much of what life asks of me, neglecting my needs hit a saturation point, and I the emotions continued to build, and my mind continued to race.

 

Eventually, it exceeded my capacity, and there I was, lying in bed, unable to downregulate, my mind speaking with thoughts like:

  • "You should be able to do it all."
  • "You should be making more money."
  • "You should be able to handle this."
  • "You shouldn't feel angry."
  • "You shouldn't feel overwhelmed."

As you can see, the "shoulds' of my inner critic were blaring on loudspeaker, and creating pressure internally.

 

In times like these, I have a few things I've found to be helpful, and I want to share them with you here.

 

First, I confronted my inner critic, and I said inside, "enough", in a kind yet firm voice to create a pattern break, and stand up to that part of myself that can be abusive at times.

 

Second, I got up, went outside, and chose to lay down on the cool grass. 

 

Third, within about 10 minutes of lying there and saying a prayer for support, I started to feel held, and things began to slow down.

 

I felt the cooling nature of the earth soothing, and opening my body and mind.

 

When things are at their most stressful, sometimes the answer is this: do nothing. Sit or lie down, and allow.

 

Sometimes it's a mix of allowing it all to happen, while at the same time being a stand for YOU. By that I mean, in this case my inner critic was actually not helpful and only exacerbating the stories and internalization of what really needed to be felt - the emotions (frustration and anger mainly in this case), and allowing them to be there and move through.

 

 As I sat up, the fourth step was I went inside my home and I did a process I guide clients through called Storywork, where you write down the specific event, situation, or moment that has an energetic grip on you, and with the breath as well as a specific structure, things then really started to slow down.

 

After this, I felt like a massive weight had been lifted in real time, and I was able to not only go to sleep, but do so feeling as if a substantial healing had taken place. 

 

Now let me ask you this, have you ever found yourself lying awake at night, unable to turn off your thoughts?

 

You’re not alone. Many men struggle with sleepless nights, and often, it’s not just about stress but deeper issues that need addressing.

 

Here's a few to consider...

 

Why Men Lie Awake:

  • Isolation: Feeling disconnected from other men, lacking deep, supportive relationships
  • Unresolved and Supressed Emotions: Bottling up feelings can create an internal pressure cooker that keeps you up.
  • Inner Critic: A harsh internal dialogue that foster self-doubt and perfectionism.
  • Unmet Expectations: The weight of societal and self-imposed expectations can lead to anxiety and restlessness.
  • Lack of Purpose: When you’re unclear about your direction in life, it’s easy for doubts and fears to surface in the quiet of the night.

 

Here's some things to also consider of what men deeply desire:

 

What Men Crave:

  • Authentic Connection: A safe space to be yourself and share your struggles without judgment.
  • Purpose and Direction: Clarity about where you’re going and why it matters.
  • Inner Peace: The ability to quiet the mind and find contentment, no matter the circumstances.
  • Emotional Freedom: The ability to express emotions openly without fear of judgment
  • Growth and Fulfillment: Opportunities to evolve, learn and reach your full potential

These challenges and desires aren’t just isolated thoughts—they’re signals that something needs attention.
