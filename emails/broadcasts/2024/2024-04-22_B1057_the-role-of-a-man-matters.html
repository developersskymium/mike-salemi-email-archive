Hey {first_name},

 

When did men start selling themselves short?

 

Look, I get it...

 

There are a variety of reasons that point to why this is happening:

  • Many men grow up without a father or father figure present, and even when they are, they often aren't emotionally available.
  • Historically, there was a shift during the industrial age, where children and adolescents didn't spend nearly as much time with their dads - observing, learning, and getting the nourishment from spending time alongside their father.
  • Society tends to label masculinity as "toxic."
  • There's even confusion about what being a man means today.

We can see that many men have lost touch with their innate masculine essence. As society at large, we've forgotten the ancient rites of passage and rituals that once guided us into manhood. We've become disconnected from our primal instincts and emotions, leading to a sense of emptiness and aimlessness.

 

The pattern I see, where many men diminish their role in the family unit and in society at large, needs to come to an end.

 

Witnessing men who are rising up, and who are each day coming to the recognition that THEY DO MATTER, and their role is crucial, is beyond inspiring. 

 

Masculine leadership, when healthy, is a gift. When embodying a healthy masculine form, you'll notice how people around you begin to relax, begin to feel safer. There's a directive force to life. There is more integrity all around.

 

A big part of how this has continued to come to my awareness at first surprised me and was not what I expected. 

 

I was actually receiving letters from wives, girlfriends, and partners expressing gratitude and awe about how their man was showing more patience with their kids, listening more with an open heart, and trying to "fix" them less. They came back with a surplus of energy and more.

 

I realized that my passion for men's work isn't just about men's work. In fact, I don't know if people really even care about men's work. But what do care about what it can do for them.

 

In order to create a deep internal shift, there are a few things that I've found helpful:

  • Exploring with curiosity and courage those core beliefs holding men back.
  • Shedding light on the stories and emotions that drive our actions.
  • Training the nervous system with dedicated practices to increase emotional capacity.
  • Crafting a safe space for growth.
  • Engaging in precise movements to boost physical strength and integrity.
  • Embracing ritual to infuse intentionality into daily life.
  • Nourishing the body and soul with a purpose.
  • Building a supportive community where egos are left at the door.

 

I won't sugarcoat it; it's not easy. But trust me, it's worth it.
