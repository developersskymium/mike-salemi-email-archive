# I feel empty

Hey {first_name},


"I feel empty..."


This was the exact phrase a former client said to me during a coaching call. He had everything society defines as success: fitness, style, cars, houses, and a new boat. He was desired by women and had many friends.

 

Yet, despite checking all these boxes, he still felt utterly “empty” inside.


For privacy, let’s call him "John."


John felt happiest when recognized for his achievements (rather than for who he was apart from them). He excelled at emulating the most successful people around him, acquiring the same material possessions and adopting their habits. However, by focusing on societal definitions of success, he never discovered what truly mattered to him.

 

He rarely let anyone into his heart by being vulnerable and saw emotions as speed bumps on his way to the top.


He emphasized who he thought he needed to be, never asking himself the critical question, “What do I really want?”


When I asked him that question, it stumped him. It was scary for him to even consider it authentically.

 

It was a blind spot.


Through guided exploration, structured embodiment practices, and coaching, we focused on opening his heart to himself and clarifying what he truly wanted in life: to bring his authentic self to the world and a future relationship.
 

While John's story is unique, parts of it may resonate with you. We all have core beliefs formed in childhood that get reinforced over time. Deep down, we all want to feel loved, adequate, safe, and complete, knowing that we are enough. The truth is, we are already that; we've just been conditioned to forget it.


In my opinion, life’s work is to continually return to the visceral knowing of our essential nature.


Working with clients like John is confirmation of why I do what I do.


If you resonate with John’s story, I’d love to hear about it.


Wishing you all the best,
Mike
