B170 · [2nd Workout] 3 Strange Fitness Lessons I've Learned From Paul Chek

Hi {first_name},

 

So you and I can probably agree...Paul is not like anybody else out there.

I mean… 

He's probably the only guy who can lift mammoth-sized stones...

Create works of art that move you to tears...

And talk about the meaning of our existence for what seems like an eternity.

All while wearing a pair of farmer pants and looking good while doing it.

Ok...that last bit might be a bit of stretch depending on your idea of fashion :).

But all joking aside, I've been blessed to know and train with Paul for over 7 years.

And what a magnificent and wild journey it's been (so far).

He's taught me countless lessons.

Some I applied and instantly benefited from (like the concept of working in mentioned last time).

Some I didn't really "get" until months or years down the line when I was ready to fully receive them.

Some I still meditate on to this very day.

Out of these, there are a few that really surprised me.

#1) It doesn't take extensive workouts and hours in the gym to be built like a Beast.  

Let's face it, Paul looks and moves like a beast.

I can safely say he would outperform many elite athletes out there, especially in terms of functional strength.  

Yet...I've never seen him spend more than an hour in the gym for a training session.

So what is it that separates him from anyone else?  

He is very purposeful in his training.

A lot of times, his time in the gym would only be 30 minutes.

 

But none of those 30 minutes are wasted.

 

Every rep is intentional.

 

Every set is intentional.

 

Every minute is intentional.

 

When Paul is training, there is nothing else on his mind.

 

One final thing he does is incorporate movement and strength building into the rest of his day.

 

It's not broken down into separate phases of his day...

It's a Lifestyle.

 

You can see this when he lifts and stacks stones.

 

Each session is a meditation and workout combined.

 

I mean…

 

It sort of has to be when you're lifting stones that are over 200 pounds :).


#2) It takes time to truly learn your athletes.

Even though Paul's initial assessment and program design for my body took several *hours*...

It took him another 3-5+ months(!) to deeply learn and understand my body's response to training.

I know...

That long and is there really that much of a difference?

Yep.

I could literally feel the workouts becoming more effective week by week.

Not only did my body get stronger...

I was able to learn to really feel my own body...something that only comes when you are intentional and purposeful.

I've definitely realized that coaching is as much art as it is science.

Which brings me to...

#3) Art can have a profound impact on healing and your development as an athlete.

 

Before working with Paul initially years ago in 2013...

 

I didn't know how much he used art as healing work in his personal life and professional practice.

 

You could even see a taste of that in the first workout video where we did an art piece together.

 

Why does this matter...?

 

Exercise, like art, is an outward expression of who you are.

 

So...

 

Any blocks or restrictions in either one could impact the other.

 

I've personally gotten a lot of joy, healing, connection, and exploration from the art I've created, both with Paul...

 

And the work I've done at home.

 

So how can you apply it in your own life and training?

 

Produce something every single day. 

 

It could be anything.

 

A stick figure drawing...

 

Drawing shapes and coloring those shapes...

 

A grand and epic painting of the beauty that surrounds all of us...

 

Anything.

 

The most important part of it is doing it with...

 

No judgment.

 

Give yourself permission to just create and express yourself.

Let your inner child just be.

 

If you find that you don't have the time to do it every day, 

 

You might find it helpful to incorporate it as a set in your workout...

 

Which is something that Paul and I actually did in our first workout together (again here's the link for that).
