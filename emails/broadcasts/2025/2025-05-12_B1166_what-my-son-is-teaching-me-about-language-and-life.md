# What my son is teaching me about language (and life)

Hi {first_name},




When I stepped into fatherhood, I thought I’d be the one doing the teaching.




Turns out—I’m the one being taught.




Every day, my son Luca reveals something new. Sometimes it’s subtle. Sometimes it stops me in my tracks. But what’s becoming more and more clear is just how much this experience is shaping me, not the other way around.




Lately, what’s been coming into sharp focus is how deeply our language—especially the kind we don’t even realize we use—shapes the world around us.




Not just in what we say.
But in how we think, choose, and act.





Because while embodiment is paramount…
Language is one of the key levers that either keeps us stuck or moves us forward.




Now that Luca’s speech is really coming online, I’m watching him pick up not only new words, but new patterns. From me.




Just the other day I asked him if he wanted to go to the park.
He paused and said:
“I guess so… maybe.”




Not a strong yes. Not a no.
Just a soft, uncertain middle ground.
And I could hear it clearly—it wasn’t coming from him.
It was a regurgitation of my words. My patterns.




I’ve used those same phrases in passing, without even thinking.
And now here they were, reflected back to me in his tiny voice.




He’s not just learning to speak.
He’s learning how I speak.
He’s picking up on the way I make decisions.
How I express uncertainty.
How I carry myself in small, day-to-day moments.




And it’s not just about him.

It’s about the reflection he’s giving me—of my own internal dialogue.
My own unconscious language habits.
The ones that sound like:

“I should probably…”
“I think I can…”
“It’s kind of hard…”

Left unexamined, those small phrases become the script.
And the script becomes the story.
And the story becomes the life we live inside of.




This is one of the deepest gifts of fatherhood.
It’s not just about protecting or providing.
It’s about seeing—really seeing—the patterns we carry, and doing the work to shift them so the next generation doesn’t have to.




Because we don’t just pass down traits.
We pass down tone.
We pass down tendencies.
We pass down storylines.




The stories we don’t resolve get recycled.
The words we speak, especially the ones spoken on autopilot, become the environment our children grow up in.




And that’s why I’ve been paying close attention to my language.
Not just for him, but for me.
For the younger version of me that didn’t have this level of awareness.
For the man I’m becoming by choosing a different script.




So here’s something simple to try this week:

Speak like someone is learning how to live from your example.
Because someone is.
Whether it’s your child, your partner, your team—or the younger part of yourself still waiting to be led with clarity.




The words you use shape the space you live in.
Choose them with care.
Choose them with presence.
And when you can—choose the ones that move you (and those you love) forward.




If you ever want support in working through your language or your stories, just reach out—this is the kind of work I love doing with clients.





In Health & Happiness,
