B1132 · The Language of Pain vs. The Language of Power

Hi {first_name},

 

Imagine this:

 

You wake up already feeling behind. The weight of your to-do list presses on your chest before your feet hit the floor. By the time the day is over, you’ve given everything you’ve got—to work, to your family, to the endless demands pulling you in a thousand directions.

 

And yet, deep down, there’s this gnawing frustration: “Why does it feel like no matter how much I do, it’s never enough?”

 

You tell yourself things will get better once you check that next box or hit that next milestone. You keep pushing, thinking, “If I can just do more, give more, or try harder, things will finally fall into place.”

 

But here’s the hard truth: that cycle never ends.

 

Beneath it all is often something deeper—a pattern that keeps so many people stuck and exhausted.

 

This is what I call The Victim Trap.

 

It doesn’t look like blame at first—it looks like justification. It feels like:

  • “I have to do it all because no one else will.”
  • “If my circumstances were different, I could finally [insert goal here].”
  • “I’m so drained, but I don’t want to disappoint anyone.”

The Victim Trap is sneaky because it convinces you that you’re doing everything right. But in reality, it’s pulling you further from your power.

 

Here’s the hard truth: when you live in the Victim Trap, you hand over your control. Life happens to you, not through you.

 

And it all starts with your words.

 

As mentor and friend Mark England teaches, the language you use creates your reality. Victim language is heavy, constricting, and full of absolutes like:

  • “I have to.”
  • “They made me feel this way.”
  • “There’s nothing I can do.”

But here’s the good news: just as your words can trap you, they can also set you free.

 

Here’s how to start breaking the cycle:

  1. Slow down your words. Write down the story you’re telling yourself about your current situation. Get it out of your head and onto paper.
  2. Look for absolutes. Highlight the have to’s, can’t’s, and should’s. These are the red flags of victim language.
  3. Reframe your story. Replace those absolutes with empowering choices. For example:
  • Victim: “I have to stay late because no one else will do their part.”
  • Empowered: “I’m choosing to stay late because this project matters to me, and I can also set boundaries moving forward.”

This isn’t about ignoring challenges or pretending everything is easy. It’s about reclaiming your power to choose how you respond to life.

 

Every powerful man I’ve met has mastered one thing: the ability to recognize his patterns and take action to change them.
