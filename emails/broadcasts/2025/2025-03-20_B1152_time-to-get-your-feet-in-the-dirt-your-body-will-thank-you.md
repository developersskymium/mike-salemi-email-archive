# Time to Get Your Feet in the Dirt (Your Body Will Thank You)

Hi {first_name},

 

If you're like me, you spend so much time managing life—emails, schedules, notifications.

 

And if we're not mindful, we forget what it feels like to be in life.

 

Nature doesn’t run on to-do lists. The river isn’t rushing to its next meeting. The mountain isn’t trying to be anything other than what it is.

 

And at your deepest core, neither are you.

 

The problem is, most men don’t realize how disconnected they’ve become until they step outside of it. Until they take their shoes off, put their feet in the dirt, and feel something they haven’t felt in years—presence.

 

Science backs this up. Studies show that time in nature lowers cortisol, improves nervous system function, and enhances cognitive clarity. But you don’t need a study to tell you that something shifts when you spend real time outdoors—away from distractions, fully immersed.

 

When you step outside of the noise, something shifts. Your breath slows. Your body settles. And the stress you’ve been carrying starts to feel… like it was never yours to hold in the first place.

 

This isn’t just relaxation. It’s a full-system reset.

 

One practice I've committed to at least once a day is getting outside with my feet in the dirt while breathing consciously.

 

Sometimes this happens when the sun rises. Other times, I eat my lunch outside. And sometimes, I’ll just hang with my pup after work. It could be as little as 60 seconds or as long as an hour or two, depending on my day. But no matter what, I make sure it happens at least once.

 

What I’ve noticed is that my body and spirit crave more of it. That’s why, often in between meetings, I’ll head outside—even for a few seconds. Think of it like the minimum effective dose.

 

I’ve never given someone a 60-second feet-in-the-dirt challenge and had them regret it. Every single time, they’ve said how good it felt. And more often than not, they stayed longer and found creative ways to be outside—to ground, reset, and break out of old patterns.

 

But if you can’t see yourself stepping away from the computer, trust me—start small. Just 60 seconds, at the same time each day for a minimum of 7 days. See what kind of momentum and benefits that creates over time.

 

Try it. You won’t regret it.

 

In Health & Happiness,

 

Mike Salemi
