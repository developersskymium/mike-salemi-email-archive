Hi {first_name},

 

This past weekend, I co-led a men’s workshop deep in the redwoods—a space I return to again and again because it strips away the noise and reveals what’s real. But this time, something was different.

 

The land itself felt like it was in transition—cloudy, quiet, holding something just beneath the surface. And as I spoke with the men who showed up, I saw the same.

 

There was a theme of being caught in the loop of working hard, providing, and achieving—yet feeling deeply frustrated and angry when it led to undesirable and even dysfunctional results. Some felt like they were failing as fathers or partners. Others were realizing that success on paper wasn’t bringing them the joy, clarity, or fulfillment they had expected. And yet, a part of them kept repeating the cycle—because they had grown so accustomed to the familiar feeling of "failing" that they had become addicted to the shame that came with it.

Before hiking in silence, I shared something simple but powerful:

 

The difference between processing vs. dwelling.

 

Dwelling is the downward spiral—shame, blame, control, running the same script over and over again. Processing is different. It’s about fully feeling what’s real while staying connected to yourself, your heart, and your direction forward—not getting stuck in the past.

 

I felt it in myself, too. My son had been sick with a cold for weeks, sleep had been rough, responsibilities shifted—and yet, just five hours in nature, breathing, moving, and being with men, gave me the space to recalibrate.

 

This is what so many men are missing.

 

Which is why, this May, I’m hosting The Primal Reset Retreat—a four-day experience in Mount Shasta, CA in collaboration with Tim Corcoran, a masterful guide with over 50 years of experience leading men through wilderness rites of passage.

 

We’ll be:

  • Learning RAD survival skills like fire-making and primal movement—reconnecting with our bodies and instincts.
  • Going on a mini half day vision quest—a guided experience that presses pause on the relentless pace of life so you can hear what’s next.
  • Sitting around the fire in deep conversation—no filters, no distractions, just real brotherhood.
  • Breaking the patterns that have kept you stuck

This isn’t a retreat where you check out of life. It’s one where you step back in—with clarity, presence, and a fire inside you that you haven’t felt in a long time.
