# How I Build Grip, Rotation, and Real-World Power Daily

Hi {first_name},

 

Most training programs focus on linear movements—squats, deadlifts, presses. And for good reason. These lifts help build the foundational strength we need for overall durability and power.

 

They align with many of our body's primal movement patterns—push, pull, squat, hinge, lunge, twist, and gait.

 

But here’s the thing… one of these patterns is almost always neglected.

 

Rotation.

 

Why Rotational Strength Matters More Than You Think

Most of the power we generate—whether in sports, daily movement, or simply staying strong for life—comes from rotation. It’s the missing link between raw strength and real-world functionality.

 

Think about it: when you throw a bag of dog food into the back of your car, catch yourself from slipping on a wet floor, carry a heavy suitcase up a flight of stairs, toss your kid in the air and catch them, or pull something down from a high shelf while rotating your torso, your body isn’t working in isolated, linear patterns—it’s relying on rotational strength, core stability, and grip endurance to generate and control force efficiently.

 

Yet, most training programs overemphasize sagittal-plane lifts (squats, deadlifts, presses) while neglecting transverse-plane strength, where true athleticism and injury prevention are built. If you’re only training in straight lines, you’re leaving a massive gap in your strength, mobility, and durability.

 

For those who understand functional movement, rotation isn’t optional—it’s foundational. Without it, strength becomes rigid, power lacks fluidity, and injury risk skyrockets.

CHEK Faculty Matt Wallden states that 80% of power in sports comes from rotational force.
Back injuries account for nearly 20% of all workplace injuries, often due to improper lifting techniques and uncontrolled twisting.
About 80% of Americans will experience at least one bout of back pain in their life. 

And Paul Chek says it best—“You can't fire a cannon from a canoe.” If your core can’t stabilize and transfer rotational power, your strength won’t carry over into real-world movement.

 

What I Personally Do

Well, one of the things I can tell you is I keep portable training tools on hand. I have a Bulgarian Bag in my office and use it almost daily. If you’ve never trained with one, it’s a tool built for rotation, grip endurance, and muscular stamina—all in one.

 

As a father, this isn’t just theory—it’s real life. When I pick up my son, carry him on one side, twist to put him in the car, or chase him around, my body isn’t just moving up and down—it’s shifting, rotating, and adjusting in multiple directions. Training this way means I can do all of it without pain, stiffness, or feeling worn down.

 

How You Can Start

Even just 5 minutes of Bulgarian bag training will:

Develop grip endurance (your grip is your nervous system’s first line of strength).
Strengthen your trunk for real-world power (not just static planks).
Improve spinal movement and durability (key for injury prevention).
Increase conditioning without beating up your joints.

The Bulgarian Bag in Action

When I teach certifications on the bag (by the way, at the end of 2025, I’ll be running Level I & Level II Bulgarian Bag Certifications in California), one thing always stands out—how much intelligent training you can get out of such a simple tool.

 

We put in a ton of practice. The kind that builds true functional strength—rotational control, grip endurance, and core stability.

 

And the best part? Participants wake up the next day feeling better, not wrecked. More mobile, strong, and connected, instead of beaten down. That’s the kind of training that translates beyond the gym and into life.
