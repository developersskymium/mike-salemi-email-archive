B1148 · What If One Word Could Change Everything?

Hi {first_name},

 

Mindset. It’s a word that gets thrown around all the time, but most definitions overcomplicate it.

 

Mark England, a friend and mentor, has the simplest, cleanest definition I’ve ever heard:

 

Mindset is the story we tell ourselves, and the words we use to tell that story.

 

That’s it.

 

And here’s the thing—we are always telling ourselves a story.

 

A story about the past.
A story about the future.
A story about why we’re stuck.

A story about how our partner criticizes us.
A story about fear, judgment, anger, resentment.

 

Sometimes these stories serve us. Sometimes they keep us trapped.

 

If you want to experience a shift in mindset, don’t start with “thinking positive.”


Start with the raw materials of your stories—the words themselves.

 

A Simple Game

Today, I want to invite you to a simple, but powerful exercise. This isn’t about fake positivity. It’s about precision and awareness—seeing how even the smallest change in language can shift how you feel, breathe, and move through the world.

 

To keep things simple, I'm going to pull out one particular sentence that came up recently with a client, and we are going to work with it together in realtime.

 

Here’s how it works:

  • Read each sentence out loud.
  • Pause and check in with yourself briefly: How does it feel? What's the vibe?
  • Move to the next sentence and repeat. Notice even the micro shifts - in your breath, your posture, or your internal dialogue. 
  • Bonus: Take one full breath before reading the next statement

 

The Language Game

 

READ: "When will I ever just get over this fear?"

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 
READ: "When will I get over this fear?"

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 

READ: "What if I get over this fear?"

Stop. Check In. Breathe.


READ: "What if I get over this story?"

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 

READ: "I could get over this story."

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 

READ: "I can get over this story."

Stop. Check In. Breathe.


READ: "I can welcome parts of this story."

Stop. Check In. Breathe.


READ: "I can welcome parts of me."

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 

READ: "I can welcome me."

Stop. Check In. Breathe.

 

Did you notice any difference?

 

At the start, the words likely felt heavy - restrictive, trapped, almost impossible.

 

As you moved down, the weight started to lift. The breath slowed. A subtle shift happened.

 

The fear is still there, but instead of fighting it, you may find yourself expanding beyond it, welcoming more of your experience. 

 

This is one small example of how you can work with language - especially in the context of a story where there is a lot of energetic charge connected to it. 

 

We’re not forcing positivity here. We’re choosing our words with intention, slowing down, breathing deep, and exploring new possibilities incrementally - without being overly attached. 

 

And in doing so, we create space, clarity, and new possibilities.

 

If you did indeed give this game a try, reply and let me know what you noticed, I'd love to hear about it.
