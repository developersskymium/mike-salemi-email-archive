About three years ago, I was visiting friends in Texas. We ended up at a small gathering at one of their friends’ homes—a beautiful, upscale high-rise downtown. The food was amazing, and the conversations were engaging and nourishing.

 

But there was one moment from that night that has stuck with me ever since.

 

A couple at the gathering, married for over 15 years, mentioned something that completely caught me off guard:

 

They said they’ve never had a fight in all those years.

 

First thought? No way—that’s gotta be BS.

 

Second thought? Judgment time. I was secretly rolling my eyes and thinking, How is that even possible?

 

Third thought? They must be delusional. Harsh, I know, but that’s where my mind went.

 

You see, I couldn’t fathom a relationship without conflict. Not then, not now.

 

Now, maybe they were using the word "fight" differently than I would, but let’s be real—if there’s no conflict, something’s likely being swept under the rug. And that’s where the real trouble begins.

 

Why Conflict Matters

 

Here’s the thing: I believe conflict is crucial in relationships.

 

The key is fighting fair—approaching conflict productively and with love. Yet many men struggle with this. Whether it’s the "nice guy" tendency to avoid conflict altogether or passive-aggressive behavior that sidesteps confrontation, these patterns can lead to disconnection and resentment.

 

Growing up, I deeply resonated with the "nice guy" role. I’d avoid conflict because it felt unsafe, always trying to spin things positively to keep the peace. But what I’ve learned—through my own growth and in my relationship with Lauren—is that avoiding conflict doesn’t keep the peace. It just delays the storm.

 

Building Healthy Conflict Skills

 

Here are a few things that have helped me (and might help you, too):

 

1. Set Rules for Conflict

Agree on boundaries with your partner. For example, no name-calling or cheap shots, ever

2. Learn Productive Frameworks

Tools like nonviolent communication, Imago dialogue, or authentic relating can help clear emotional charges, avoid judgment, and create space for repair.

3. Master the Art of Repair

Repairing after conflict is one of the most important skills you can develop in a committed relationship. It’s not about avoiding conflict but addressing it with the intention of strengthening the connection.

 

The Real Problem with Avoiding Conflict

 

The desire to "keep the peace" often backfires. When we avoid addressing issues that need attention, we miss an opportunity to build deeper intimacy and connection. Worse, we reinforce dysfunctional patterns and allow resentment to grow.

 

And resentment? That’s poison for any relationship. It creates emotional distance, breeds miscommunication, and erodes trust and intimacy over time.

 

A Commitment to Love

 

Reflecting on that story from Texas, I still believe conflict is healthy—and necessary—in relationships.

 

But as men, it’s up to us to lead with love, even in the messy, uncomfortable moments. When handled productively, conflict can elevate the entire relationship.
