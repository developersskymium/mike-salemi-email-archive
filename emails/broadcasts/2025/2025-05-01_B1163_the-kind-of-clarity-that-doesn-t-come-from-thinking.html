B1163 · The kind of clarity that doesn’t come from thinking

Hi {first_name},

 

Ever get to the end of the day and realize you were moving all day…
…but not actually connected to any of it?

 

You knocked out the tasks.
You showed up for your people.
You kept it all moving.

 

But somewhere in the background, there’s this low hum of disconnection — like your body’s in one place, your mind’s in another, and your spirit’s… on hold.

 

I know that feeling well.

 

And I’ve learned something that’s been opening up new space for me — slowly, quietly, but powerfully.

 

Not every answer comes through thinking.
Some only come when you stop long enough to listen.

 

I want to share something I’ve been exploring — a simple practice called the sit spot.


It’s something I’ve seen Tim Corcoran teach with reverence, and something I’ve started integrating into my own rhythm — not perfectly, but intentionally.

 

Here’s what it is:

You pick a spot in nature — ideally the same place each time.
You go there. And you sit.

That’s it.

 

You could journal, track your breathing, etc.
However, there are going to be many times when the deepest medicine is simply observing.
Presence. Listening. Being.

 

At first, it’s uncomfortable.
I’ve noticed my mind wanting to wander, to fill the space.
But over time — even just a few sessions in — something begins to shift.

 

The pace of the land takes over.
Your breath finds a different rhythm.
And what’s most alive in you starts to surface.

 

Tim teaches that the sit spot isn’t about “tuning out.”
It’s about tuning in — to the land, yes, but also to the parts of you that get buried in the pressure to perform.

 

The more I’ve leaned into it, the more I’ve realized how rare it is for men — especially high-performing men — to simply sit and receive.

But that’s where a deeper kind of clarity lives.

 

Not the kind that comes from a list.
The kind that arrives — when you finally give it space.

 

 

In Health & Happiness,

 

Mike Salemi
Performance Specialist, Men's Leadership Coach, & Host of The Path Podcast
