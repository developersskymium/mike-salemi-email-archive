B1168 · The Practice Ambitious Men Avoid (That Changes Everything)

Hi {first_name},

 

Stillness is one of the most overlooked practices in men’s development.

 

When people talk about “doing the work,” they usually point to emotional expression, i.e. feeling your feelings, getting vulnerable, softening. And for many men, especially those raised to believe emotions make you weak, that work is absolutely necessary.

 

But feeling alone isn’t the full picture. It’s often incomplete without something more foundational: stillness.

 

Stillness is not passive. It’s not zoning out. It’s not a spa-day luxury. It’s a masculine discipline. A way to build presence, sharpen your focus, and reconnect to what actually matters. It’s about slowing down enough to meet what’s real, without escaping or distracting.

 

The truth is, most ambitious men resist stillness. They feel like they’re wasting time. Like they’re losing ground. Like if they’re not moving, they’re falling behind.

 

But here’s what I’ve seen, especially in the men who committed to this inside the men's group I assist alongside Ted Riter called The Wisdom Circle over the last six months. The ones who chose meditation or a daily stillness practice are showing up in a completely different way.

 

They’re calmer. More grounded.
They’re having the hard conversations. Following through.
They’re leading with clarity, not chaos.
They’re not reaching for energy drinks or blood sugar spikes to stay in motion.
They’re not reactive. They’re steady.

 

And it didn’t take hours of meditation to get there. They just built the muscle one small rep at a time.

 

Here are two simple ways to start your own stillness practice:

1. Sit Completely Still

  • Find a quiet space, ideally outside.
  • Sit upright and set a timer for 3 minutes.
  • Don’t move. No scratching, fidgeting, adjusting.
  • Just breathe. Listen. Feel what’s present.
  • When your mind wanders (and it will), come back to stillness.

 

This might be the hardest thing you do all day, and the most powerful. You’re not trying to clear your mind. You’re training your nervous system to stay with what is.

 

2. 3-Minute Body Scan

  • Sit or lie down somewhere comfortable with your eyes closed.
  • Bring your attention to the crown of your head.
  • Slowly scan down your body, part by part: jaw, chest, belly, legs, feet.
  • At each point, simply ask: What do I feel here?
  • You’re not trying to fix anything. Just notice.
  • If your mind drifts, gently return to the body.

 

This reconnects you to sensation. It gets you out of your head and into your body, which is where your actual power lives.

 

Stillness builds the kind of leadership most men never access.

 

It creates space to hear what’s really going on inside you. It builds the capacity to hold yourself steady, and to hold those around you when it matters most.

 

If you want to lead with presence, start here.

 

In Health & Happiness,
