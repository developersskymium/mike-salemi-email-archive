Hi {first_name},

 

A few weeks ago, I sat down with my journal and asked myself, What’s the one thing I can focus on right now that would make the biggest difference in my life?

 

It sounds simple, but getting to the answer wasn’t easy. My mind immediately started juggling a dozen different ideas—family commitments, work projects, personal goals. It’s like part of me wanted to hold onto the belief that I could tackle everything at once.

 

But here’s the truth I’ve learned the hard way: trying to do everything usually means doing nothing well.

 

I’ve been familiar with the concept of focusing on “the one thing” for years (there’s even a great book by that name by Gary Keller and Jay Papasan), but living it? That’s where it gets tricky.

 

Here’s the core idea: Your one thing is the single, most important task that drives everything else forward. It’s the action that creates momentum, clarity, and results.

 

When you focus on your one thing, everything else either falls into place or becomes unnecessary.

 

For me, it took some deep reflection to figure out what my one thing was in different areas of my life.

 

In my work, it’s simple: how many genuine connections and offers I make daily. That’s the clearest way to drive financial growth, serve more men, and make the kind of impact I care about.

 

In my relationship, Lauren and I have committed to tackling something we’ve avoided for years—our money patterns. We’ve signed up for a financial course together, which has sparked some tough but transformative conversations.

 

And in my personal life, it’s my daily practice: 10 minutes minimum of movement, meditation, and breathwork. Without that foundation, I’d be running on fumes and wouldn’t be able to show up fully for anyone else.

 

Now, none of this clarity would have been possible if I stayed stuck in the biggest productivity lie: the myth of multitasking.

 

The Lie of Multitasking

Multitasking sells itself as a productivity hack. It makes you feel like you’re getting more done. But here’s what’s really happening:

  • You’re not multitasking—you’re task-switching. Your brain isn’t built to focus on two complex tasks at once. Instead, it jumps back and forth, losing efficiency and energy every time.
  • You’re draining your mental energy. Each switch burns more focus, leaving you tired, distracted, and more likely to make mistakes.
  • You’re stuck in shallow work. Multitasking keeps you skimming the surface, never diving deep enough to create real progress or results.

 

In fact, studies show that multitasking can reduce your productivity by up to 40%. Think about that—almost half your energy and time wasted because your attention is divided.

 

So, what’s the alternative?

 

It’s focusing on your one thing. Instead of spreading your energy across ten different tasks, you zero in on the one that truly moves the needle.

 

Here’s how to get started:

  1. Ask the question: What’s the one thing I can do today that makes everything else easier or unnecessary? I would suggest doing some type of grounding practice right before this, and getting as still and quiet as possible to tune into your deeper inner wisdom.
  2. Block out distractions: Turn off your notifications, close unnecessary tabs, and commit to working deeply on your one thing. I enjoy creating uninterrupted blocks of time during the day for my one thing and daily "big 3" in my calendar.
  3. Trust the process: By focusing on less, you’ll achieve more—and feel more energized doing it. It does take some self-restraint to curb the desire to dive into a million things, all of which appear as important. Yet, I promise you focusing on what's actually important, rather than urgent will go longer than you might think.

 

The key is to remind yourself: doing more isn’t the goal. Doing what matters is.
